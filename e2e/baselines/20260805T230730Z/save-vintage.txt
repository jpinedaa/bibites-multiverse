The token is the request time in epoch milliseconds:
  t1785807598756880034 -> 2026-08-04T01:39:58.756Z   (slot 1 save)
  t1785807599306059541 -> 2026-08-04T01:39:59.306Z   (slot 3 save)
That sequence opened with a census at t1785807597063 (01:39:57.063Z). T0's own
saves were t1785807572721 (01:39:32.721Z) and t1785807573490
(01:39:33.490Z), which is why the zips in the T0 directory are a different
file: a FOURTH census/count/save sequence ran 25 seconds after T0's and
overwrote both saves in place. Nothing saved either world after that — these
are the last two [M2-CMD] save lines in either log, and both logs run on for
another 12.5 hours.

## 4. Cross-check: the population in the zip is the population the log
##    reported at that simulated minute

slot 3 is exact. The zip is at simulated minute 584.7 and reports 26 living
organisms; crossing-series-slot3.csv reports population=26 at minutes 584,
585 and 586. slot 1's zip is at minute 632.7 with 26 living, and the series
sits between 23 and 28 across minutes 626-636. The zips are exactly where
their timestamps say they are.

## 5. No autosave exists to fall back on

autosaveFolder: /mnt/c/Users/j_jor/AppData/LocalLow/The Bibites/The Bibites/Savefiles/Autosaves
  *.zip files present: 0
This is what the T0 report predicted: DevCommands.DisableAutosaveOnce turns
the game's autosave off at runtime for every instance started with
MULTIVERSE_CMD_FILE, so the rig owns every save. It kept the overnight run
safe from a scene reload, and it is also the reason there is no end-state
save: with autosave off and the rig sending no save before the windows
closed, the last in-memory state of both worlds was discarded on exit.
